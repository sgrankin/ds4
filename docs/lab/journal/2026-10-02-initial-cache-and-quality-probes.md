# Exact frontiers, approximate suffixes and quality probes

The first cache experiments showed that an exact prefix checkpoint can avoid rebuilding unchanged history. Reusing a suffix after an edit saves more work, but also keeps information from the old context. Low average distribution drift and a correct answer do not prove that deleted information has been forgotten.

## Context and setup

These 2026-10-02 experiments used the original local DeepSeek V4.1 Flash Q2 model on an M5 Max with 128 GiB RAM and SSD-streamed experts. This is not the later 1017 checkpoint. The quality probe compared full-vocabulary logits on identical teacher-forced histories: delta-NLL measures the change in reference-token likelihood, and KL measures distribution drift. Neither establishes practical task quality. Generated answers and explicit facts were checked separately; one-off timings below are diagnostic, not five-sample performance comparisons.

An exact frontier restores state at the longest matching prefix and recomputes the rest. In an aligned 2,816-token middle-edit fixture with a 2,048-token retained prefix, mean edit time was 59.026 s fresh and 24.362 s restored; all 129,280 logits, serialized graph state and four continuation rows matched. Snapshot save cost 12.396 ms. This same-process ABBA establishes the result for that fixture, not general agent throughput. [Frontier report](../../../speed-bench/experiments/context-frontier-20261002/README.md).

## What suffix reuse preserves

Suffix reuse instead grafts old state after recomputing an edited middle. It has to coordinate raw windows, compressed and index state, unfinished pair pooling and positional rotations; moving ordinary KV rows alone is insufficient. In a same-position edited-code fixture, both fresh and reuse answered `3141`; edit times were 40.454 and 17.551 s, with mean KL 0.0000133 over three answer rows. A 128-token deletion required position repair; both answers remained correct, with 4.626 versus 3.105 s and KL 0.0000113. These individual timings are not balanced comparisons. [Semantic and relocation evidence](../../../speed-bench/ideas/evidence/suffix-semantic-and-relocation-20261002.json).

The redaction tests exposed a different failure: both paths answered `REDACTED`, yet the old secret's complete-answer probability rose from 2.57e-13 fresh to 5.08e-5 with reuse. A 64-token fresh tail reduced it to 4.97e-12 in that fixture, but a separate calibrated lookup still raised the deleted code's probability from 1.54e-15 to 1.89e-6 with 128 fresh tail tokens, even though both paths answered `UNKNOWN`. Fresh tails reduce stale influence in some cases, not guarantee forgetting. The earlier invalid retrieval control was excluded. [Calibrated lookup and physical-wrap evidence](../../../speed-bench/ideas/evidence/suffix-record-x-wrap-20261002/compact-summary.json).

The live-context test removed 1,018 tokens, retained the required fact and answered `CODE=2718`. A three-turn agent test changed the fact and answered `3141`, reusing 556 suffix tokens while evaluating 476 fresh; its 24.878 s graft interval was unpaired. The original harness returned code 3 because it expected a save that headless stdin never requested. A separate idle-worker save test wrote text without reusable KV, but did not test reopening. [CLM test](../../../speed-bench/experiments/clm-live-context-20261002/README.md), [agent result](../../../speed-bench/ideas/evidence/agent-suffix-native-three-turn-20261002/NATIVE_THREE_TURN_RESULTS.md), [save test](../../../speed-bench/ideas/evidence/agent-suffix-native-persistence-20261002/NATIVE_PRIMARY_RESULTS.md).

Odd displacements needed a separate pre-pool pair tape. Its 65-token deletion pilot reused 512 tokens with a 128-token fresh tail: mean KL 0.0166751 across 17 rows, no argmax changes, and 113,379,963 bytes of owned staging under 128 MiB. A zero-edit wrap control was byte-identical, but one-token-tail inverse-parity and wrap stress cases had KL 0.1165639 and 0.05739446 respectively. These establish mechanics, not forgetting or general task accuracy. [Pilot](../../../speed-bench/ideas/evidence/suffix-pair-tape-odd65-native-20261003-v3/README.md), [identity](../../../speed-bench/ideas/evidence/suffix-pair-tape-zero-identity-native-20261003-v1/README.md), [inverse parity](../../../speed-bench/ideas/evidence/suffix-pair-tape-inverse-native-20261003-v1/README.md), [wrap](../../../speed-bench/ideas/evidence/suffix-pair-tape-wrap-native-20261003-v1/README.md).

## Reasoning interventions and operational lesson

A fixed hesitation-word penalty did not establish better reasoning. On two hard counting problems with a 1,024-token cap, native completed both correctly; lambda 1 completed one and capped the other, whether applied immediately or after 256 thinking tokens. Weaker .5 and .25 settings also left one capped. On four problems at a 2,048-token cap, the narrower penalty and native each completed only one correct answer. Native effort 25 completed two versus effort 75's one on that small set; a six-problem holdout later gave five versus four, with one versus two caps. These are small, fixed-seed task observations, not a general accuracy claim. [Hard pairs](../../../speed-bench/experiments/ds41-ssd/overthink-provenance/guarded-hard-two-summary.json), [narrow penalty](../../../speed-bench/experiments/narrow-half-counting-analysis-20261002/README.md), [effort holdout](../../../speed-bench/experiments/native-effort-holdout-20261003/README.md).

The fixed 1,024-token thinking-budget pilot reduced generated tokens from 5,985 to 4,310 while retaining two correct answers; one newly completed lattice answer was wrong and another case still capped. [Budget pilot](../../../speed-bench/experiments/reasoning-budget-pilot-20261003/compact-summary.json).

A watchdog panic occurred while a reasoning pilot and two tokenizer processes ran concurrently. The snapshot showed severe combined memory pressure and low swap space, but did not establish the watchdog's cause. A later unrelated PDF worker caused a separate pressure interruption. The lesson was one heavy model-opening job at a time under the pressure guard, judging pressure and swap rather than a fixed RAM cap. [Panic snapshot](../../../speed-bench/ideas/evidence/panic-memory.json).

## Further controls and related hypotheses

Recent exact frontiers also passed longest-prefix selection, count/byte eviction and corrupt-payload fallback, including unchanged Unicode and quoted role markers. The fresh-query admission policy rejects unsupported markers before mutation; its CPU checks are not a new actual-agent execution. [Frontier controls](../../../speed-bench/experiments/context-frontiers-20261002/README.md), [fresh-query checks](../../../speed-bench/ideas/evidence/suffix-protected-user-cpu-20261003-v1/README.md).

The October 3 suffix/CLM screen separated fixed-position isolation-policy drift from reuse drift. Fifteen compaction configurations included two wrong-answer factual-removal points; ordinary edits showed 2.5–3.3x diagnostic edit speedups. Another 881-token relocation had delta-NLL +0.56957 and KL 0.450493 on four teacher tokens, with a large error on the relevant number; it measured neither task success nor speed. [Suffix/CLM screen](../../../speed-bench/experiments/suffix-clm-hour1-20261003/README.md), [fact-likelihood case](../../../speed-bench/experiments/suffix-fact-likelihood-20261003/README.md).

Four easy penalty calibrations were correct and had identical traces at lambda 0, 0.5 and 1. A harder initial run was interrupted by the panic and its partial traces were lost, so it is not an accuracy benchmark. In the retained harder runs, the first divergence suppressed the ordinary logical token ` or`. Boosting the closing-think token by 2 after 1,024 thinking tokens saved just one token across four cases, leaving the same two correct and two capped. [Easy calibration](../../../speed-bench/experiments/ds41-ssd/overthink-calibration-20261002-1540/), [closing-token pilot](../../../speed-bench/experiments/ds41-ssd/CLOSING_FIRST_ATTEMPT_RESULT.md), [effort 25](../../../speed-bench/ideas/evidence/native-effort25-completed-20261002-v1/README.md), [effort 50](../../../speed-bench/ideas/evidence/native-effort50-completed-20261002-v1/README.md).

These directions were inspired by [Context Language Models](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.37725v1), [the overthinking penalty study](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.00206v1), and [cache-conditional experts](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.00099v2). Their published results concern other workloads or models; none establishes a DS4.1 Q2 speed or quality result.

A private worker pair-tape transaction repaired 768 pairs, reused 512 tokens after an odd 65-token deletion and kept the latest 128-token user turn fresh. Its native controls and cancellation restoration passed; mean KL was 0.020010 across 17 rows. The named save wrote no reusable KV. This established a structural transaction, not semantic forgetting or a latency promotion. [Private worker evidence](../../../speed-bench/experiments/agent-suffix-pair-native-20261003/checked-summary.json).

The later [nine-setting thinking screen](../../../speed-bench/experiments/thinking-hour4-20261003/README.md)
ran 18 attempts. All three marker penalties capped the interval task before
writing; disabling thinking passed one of two tasks and introduced an empty-interval
bug. This small October 3 screen is distinct from the later caller-selected
WRITE-stage budget experiments and did not justify a general reasoning default.
