# Native model ports and residual steering

On October 24, handwritten Zig ports qualified the ordinary single-Metal Qwen 3.8 Flash Next and GLM 5.3 Flash paths against frozen C. Residual steering was separately implemented and exercised on the four installed native families. Neither result establishes support for every legacy model, cache combination or task.

## Model ports and shared runtime

The ports at `qwen-glm-zig`, based on `030a040e`, reused the installed Q4 Qwen and Q2 GLM Flash weights and vision sidecars. Model owners cover preparation, resources, execution, prefill, decode, recurrence, speculative multi-token prediction (MTP), payloads, batching and vision. Qwen retains disk-only n-grams and pooled gated delta-net state; GLM retains its indexed attention, cache and page-in ordering. Shared headers define actual records once, while Metal/MSL primitives remain in their existing languages. At this checkpoint common engine and session services still had C owners; subsequent bounded slices replaced those owners and qualified the assembled runtime, without turning a forwarding facade into a migration. Details and revisions are in the [port report](../../../speed-bench/experiments/qwen-glm-zig-20261024/README.md) and [shared runtime report](../../../speed-bench/experiments/native-runtime-20261024/README.md).

Frozen and native frontier matrices matched bit for bit at 2047, 2048 and 2049 tokens, and vision arrays matched across seven cases. State, MTP, real Responses/WebSocket and frozen-to-native-to-frozen payload tests passed. GLM continued versus cold prefill retained an inherited numerical difference; three inherited GLM correctness problems in SSD MTP mapping and invalidation were fixed. Qwen and GLM public payload exchange does not imply automatic server SSD or suffix caching for those families.

On the 128 GiB M5 Max, short ABBA screens showed Qwen live append at 1245.23 versus 1242.27 tokens/s and GLM warm prefill at 355.10 versus 354.25 tokens/s; these were regression screens, not speedup claims. Qwen first prefill varied from 222 to 948 tokens/s across both binaries. An initial GLM global-map run was pressure-stopped and a 4096-context allocation exceeded its 110 GiB limit; qualification used the bounded fallback and a 24 GB expert cache, without relaxing the guard. Resident GLM shared-weight batch math was not exercised in that SSD-only setup.

The shared migration then moved preparation, publication, payload and snapshot I/O, selected-expert loading, page-in, session and engine lifecycle into native owners, followed by continuation, sampling, multimodal and storage policy. Combined checks covered 103 live protocol cases and model state, GPU and frozen-logit comparisons. See the report for the measured screens and the two pressure-stopped Qwen runs caused by another live Flint server; isolated continuation passed after that server stopped. Full GLM and DeepSeek Pro paths retained C and were not checkpoint-qualified by the Flash ports.

## Steering the residual stream

Change `sysvyppyvons` introduced one bank per runtime with `project` and `add` modes. The normal hook edits the post-FFN, full-block residual; an option also edits the attention residual. Each HC stream gets the same hidden-width axis independently. DeepSeek and Flint publish BF16, apply the edit, then round to BF16 again. This hook deliberately does not promise compatibility with older FFN-output vectors.

For projection, each nonzero layer row is normalized and the full user strength applies at each selected layer. Addition preserves learned row magnitudes and divides strength among nonzero rows. Zero rows mask layers. Additive banks can be combined offline with their layer budgets accounted for; one summed vector generally cannot reproduce sequential projections. The bank and mode are fixed at startup, but a live strength change affects subsequent computation; KV already computed retains the earlier steering influence. Automatic KV SSD and suffix reuse is excluded when a bank is loaded, even at zero strength; expert-weight SSD streaming remains available. [Bank conventions and extraction](../../../dir-steering/README.md) explain construction and layer masks.

Both modes passed installed DS4.1 Flash, Flint, Qwen and GLM Flash checks, including exact zero logits, independent live strengths, batches and speculative replay. Flint full eight-token packets matched scalar replay. Pro and full-GLM C paths were adapted but not validated with their checkpoints. The [steering report](../../../speed-bench/experiments/steering-20261024/README.md) preserves the revision, settings and test evidence. Its two-sample mirrored timing screens ranged from roughly -2% to +7% prefill and -0.4% to +5% decode for the shown DS4.1/Flint settings; controls also varied by a few percent, so these do not establish precise overheads or speedups. Qwen/GLM latency and long-context effects were not measured there.

Changing logits alone does not prove useful behavior. A separate four-question holdout using `b7679480` found additive +1 reduced total answer words from 660 to 420 for Flint, 787 to 467 for DS4.1 and 1361 to 582 for Qwen, with all four answers judged core-correct and coherent. Flint -1 made answers longer. Projection was not consistently directional per prompt, and strong settings produced repetition or token caps. Flint retained incorrect extra numerical claims. GLM's larger baseline timed out; one bounded arithmetic example completed correctly under addition, which is not a general quality result. Refusal, coding, tool use and long-context behavior remain unqualified. The [behavior report](../../../speed-bench/experiments/steering-behavior-20261024/README.md) contains the full answers, ceilings and reproduction.
