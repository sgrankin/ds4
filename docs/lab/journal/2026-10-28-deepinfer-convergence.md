# DeepInfer convergence: recovery and draft history

The October 27 alignment experiments compared selected DeepInfer mechanisms with the local DeepSeek runtime, favoring alignment where useful but measuring local tradeoffs. The [pinned comparison](../../models/deepseek/deepinfer/comparison.md) distinguishes public source from omitted production infrastructure. The experiments below are historical results, not claims that the two runtimes have identical cache formats or defaults.

## Candidate scoring and bounded recovery

The first one-hour slice used candidate-only scalar index scoring. On 32K and 64K teacher and output probes it was exact, and kernel checks extended through 256K. Whole first-answer decode was neutral; a warm 64K replay improved 1.68%. It remained opt-in. These early timings are diagnostic, not a five-sample performance comparison. The [alignment report](../../../speed-bench/experiments/deepinfer-alignment-20261027/README.md) gives the revisions and measurements.

The next slice tested block-only recovery, starting from `56ebae95`. It imported durable compressed and index state, clipped raw visibility, then rebuilt 128 rows by scalar execution. The prototype rebuilt all 40 raw rings, so it was not an isolated implementation of the reference encoder's sliding-window recovery. Poisoned unused rings and immutable-owner checks passed, and approximate exports were blocked. At 8K, the checkpoint shrank from 63,464,500 to 52,461,620 bytes. Three normal-reasoning answers passed; total answer time was 82.70 s, versus 63.30 s for exact-hit brackets and 127.59 s for misses. At 32K, one recent-fact case took 29.16 s recovered, versus 21.95/21.58 s exact brackets and 75.37 s missed. These are exploratory observations, not a general speed claim.

On actual baseline traces of 145 tokens across three cases, mean delta-NLL was −0.00113 nats/token and KL 0.00596; the adverse case delta-NLL was +0.00102. Reasoning lengths differed. An earlier short answer-only teacher gave a different result, so neither metric alone establishes practical quality. Full sliding-window state remained preferable for recent exact hits; bounded recovery remained a diagnostic cold-recovery direction, pending batched recovery and retention measurements. It must also be distinguished from the separate opt-in decoder replay mechanism and its own activation restrictions.

## Restoring useful drafter state

Target-only restoration saves target KV but loses the drafter's 128-row feature history. DSP1 adds a canonical trailer containing three chronological BF16 target-feature tails; projected drafter KV is rebuilt on first use. Old payloads and flag-off receivers still restore target state, and equal-length source selection prefers the useful DSP1 state. In the initial HOT0 restart test, the first constrained cycle proposed five tokens, accepted four and committed six including correction; fresh scalar teacher logits matched exactly. A capped tool request exposed the independent truncation defect described in the [interaction entry](2026-10-27-interaction-and-constraints.md).

The initial HTTP diagnostic at eager confidence 0 restored 97 cached tokens and performed 8/25 verification cycles for 32/96-token caps, versus none without the feature. Complete cases passed 7/7; ordinary, strict-tool and schema timings were mixed. These early individual timings do not establish a broad speedup.

The follow-up [five-pair report](../../../speed-bench/experiments/draft-features-five-20261028/README.md) used the ordinary confidence 0.6 policy and fresh alternating pairs. OS-warm restored complete three-task HTTP time fell from 37.533 to 30.731 s, or 18.12%; all five pairs improved by 17.65–18.85%. Outputs, arguments, reasoning and token counts matched in the measured tasks. Three cached prompts occupied 10,845,024 additional bytes. Startup and seed generation were excluded, so this is not a cold SSD or non-drafter claim. The then-selected DeepSeek fast presets enabled DSP1 with an explicit zero override; the ordinary engine default remained off.

The first packed-index producer in this sequence was a 68-byte physical row with exact decoded values and scorer/ID checks, but it was only a pilot. Its subsequent serving integration and the main-KV capacity tradeoff are described in [packed cache and kernels](2026-10-28-packed-cache-and-kernels.md).
