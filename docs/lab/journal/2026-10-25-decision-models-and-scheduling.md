# Decision models and scheduling, 25 October 2026

Native Clef decision serving became useful as a co-resident helper, but its latency and scheduling tradeoffs depend on input length and competing work. The measurements below are historical screens on an M5 Max with 128 GB; most have fewer than the five samples per side now required for comparative performance claims. They motivate the implementation choices, not a general performance guarantee.

## Context and setup

The aim was to run Clef-Flash, full Clef and Clef-Omni locally alongside a generative model, exposing the same native decision operation through SystemOne and Decisions APIs. A decision model scores choices jointly against evidence; its learned joint head produces probabilities rather than generating an answer token by token. Flash and full share a dense hybrid executor; Omni uses a sparse backbone with vision and audio components. The [Clef manual](../../models/clef/usage.md) owns current usage and limits. The [qualification report](../../../speed-bench/experiments/clef-native-20261024/README.md) records weights, revisions, fixtures and reproduction.

The initial comparison used matched weights and inputs against a local MLX reference. For the full model at 2,881 tokens, native warm latency was 3,608 ms versus MLX 3,147 ms; short 157–266-token cases were within 6% either way. Flash was roughly 3–8% slower on short inputs, while Omni was 8–12% faster. These are same-machine screens, not comparisons to hosted hardware. See [speed and methodology](../../../speed-bench/experiments/clef-native-20261024/SPEED.md).

## Findings

The GPU joint head reduced short Flash request time by about 11–14% against the CPU head. Its output and execution are documented in the [head report](../../../speed-bench/experiments/clef-native-20261024/HEAD.md). On the fixed 2,000-request Decision Index 0.2.1 sample, Flash scored 91.0% accuracy on API-Bank, 92.75% macro-F1 on BANKING77, 87.20% macro-F1 on CLINC150+OOS and 63.8% accuracy on When2Call. The first 100 choices matched same-weight MLX, with mean KL 0.0000857 and delta-NLL +0.000158 nats per decision. Full and Omni each ran a 100-request smoke; 28 BLINK vision samples per model are not a full benchmark, and vision task parity with MLX was not measured. See [quality results](../../../speed-bench/experiments/clef-native-20261024/BROAD_QUALITY.md) and [media tasks](../../../speed-bench/experiments/clef-native-20261024/MEDIA-TASKS.md).

The dense performance pass used the frozen native final-build control and the same weights. At 2,048 or more rows, expanding each matrix into reusable BF16 scratch and using matrix multiplication improved the full model's warm 2,881-token time from 3,633 to 3,144 ms, and 6,731-token time from 10,368 to 8,730 ms. Flash moved from 947 to 870 ms and 2,800 to 2,410 ms respectively. Scratch was 340 MiB for full and 192 MiB for Flash when needed. Thirty-two repeated decision vectors per model and 100 Flash responses were identical to the control. The [kernel report](../../../speed-bench/experiments/clef-native-20261024/KERNEL-PERFORMANCE.md) retains the alternatives and sample details. Omni's expert tiling and audio component changes are recorded separately in the [Omni](../../../speed-bench/experiments/clef-native-20261024/OMNI.md) and [media](../../../speed-bench/experiments/clef-native-20261024/MEDIA.md) reports.

Exact one-entry prefix reuse tested 27 requests per dense model, including changed questions, changed facts and intervening short requests. Eleven hits reduced the mixed replay from 39.07 to 21.92 seconds for full and 11.21 to 6.32 seconds for Flash. All 36 decisions and complete probability distributions matched uncached results; an early fact change missed and reversed the answer. The retained entries occupied 651.14 MiB and 321.26 MiB respectively. This is a favorable repeated-prefix workload, not free acceleration for arbitrary requests. See [prefix results](../../../speed-bench/experiments/clef-native-20261024/PREFIX-CACHE.md).

A separate contention screen split the full model's GPU work at complete layer boundaries. With a 2,881-token helper, a one-layer quantum reduced the concurrent generator's completion from 4.14 to 0.885 seconds and its longest observed gap from 3.92 to 0.323 seconds, while helper time rose from 3.99 to 4.20 seconds. Four- and eight-layer quanta gave different tradeoffs; all 25 helper probability vectors were identical. This two-repeat screen left quantum zero as the default because short-helper costs and other contention patterns were not established. [Scheduling results](../../../speed-bench/experiments/clef-native-20261024/SCHEDULING.md) include the curve and procedure.

## Conclusion

The GPU head, measured dense kernels and exact prefix reuse were adopted in their qualified scopes; cooperative layer scheduling remained opt-in. The work also fixed numerical defects, including an Omni long-input routed-row overflow, before quality qualification. Independent runtime ownership and two real DS41 engines were exercised through interleaving and closure with exact logit checks, but that does not establish arbitrary multi-generator concurrency or automatic memory admission. See the [ownership report](../../../speed-bench/experiments/clef-native-20261024/OWNERSHIP.md). Current configuration and implementation details belong to the Clef manual rather than this historical decision record.
