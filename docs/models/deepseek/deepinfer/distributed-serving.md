# Distributed serving and one-Mac equivalents

DeepInfer's distributed design combines parallel model execution with a production service that places requests, retains reusable state and survives machine failures. Its [public release](https://github.com/deepseek-ai/DeepInfer/blob/553741557a57df44a46fb3ab72b6aafeaf945337/README.md) provides offline inference and core engine features, but explicitly omits a complete KV cache system, stage disaggregation and load balancing across prefill and decode replicas. The [deployment Q&A](https://github.com/deepseek-ai/DeepInfer/blob/553741557a57df44a46fb3ab72b6aafeaf945337/doc/inference_deployment_qa.md) describes the larger production design; it is not a recipe for reconstructing all of the proprietary controllers from the released code.

For a concrete request, the documented service can match a cached prefix before assigning a prefill rank, fetch its blocks while preparing the remaining prompt, then transfer state to a decode instance. Image input can first visit a separate vision encoder. Within each model step, attention is assigned to data-parallel ranks while expert routing exchanges activations across the expert-parallel group. DS4 instead serves the request on one Mac, with local model and session ownership and a local RAM/SSD cache. This comparison describes mechanisms, not a claim that DS4 has been qualified for distributed operation. See the [comparison overview](comparison.md), [model implementation](../implementation.md), [Apple Silicon guide](../apple-silicon.md), and [state-cache recipe](../../../../recipes/03-state-cache.md).

## Attention data parallelism and expert parallelism (P1)

Attention data parallelism (DP) places the attention work for independent requests on different ranks; each rank has the attention model needed for its own requests. Expert parallelism (EP) distributes the mixture-of-experts weights across ranks: a token's router selects logical experts, its activation is dispatched to their owners, and the results are combined back into the originating computation. These are different axes even when the group sizes are equal. Tensor parallelism (TP), by contrast, splits individual tensor operations across devices; it is not synonymous with either DP or EP. The production description states attention DP equals EP, with prefill EP/DP 2–8 and decode EP/DP 64–128; it says TP and context parallelism are not currently used. Wide EP reduces expert weight traffic per decode step but adds communication and depends on balanced routing. These configurations and reported throughput are for the upstream cluster, not the local quant or Mac. [Deployment parallelism](https://github.com/deepseek-ai/DeepInfer/blob/553741557a57df44a46fb3ab72b6aafeaf945337/doc/inference_deployment_qa.md#what-parallelism-is-used), [communication operations](https://github.com/deepseek-ai/DeepInfer/blob/553741557a57df44a46fb3ab72b6aafeaf945337/deepinfer/model_executor/parallel_utils/communication_op.py).

On DS4, grouped token work and reuse of locally loaded experts can improve locality, but no network dispatch is needed for its one-GPU serving path. Streaming or demand-loading experts from local SSD addresses limited memory capacity; it does not create extra expert compute owners or reproduce the throughput of wide EP. See [routing and Metal execution](../apple-silicon.md#routing-and-metal-execution).

## Balanced context parallelism for long prompts (P2)

Context parallelism (CP) splits the positions of a single long sequence across ranks, unlike attention DP, which assigns separate requests to ranks. Causal attention gives later positions more history to attend to, so a simple contiguous split can leave the late rank with more work. DeepInfer's public mirror preparation pairs an early and a late partition on each rank, constructs forward and reverse indices and attention bounds, and its CP communication supports gathering the needed data. For example, with two ranks and four equal pieces, rank 0 receives pieces 0 and 3, and rank 1 receives pieces 1 and 2. Padding and compressed-key boundaries also matter in the actual implementation. [Mirror reference](https://github.com/deepseek-ai/DeepInfer/blob/553741557a57df44a46fb3ab72b6aafeaf945337/deepinfer/model_executor/layers/tilelang_ops/prepare_cp_mirror.py), [CP metadata](https://github.com/deepseek-ai/DeepInfer/blob/553741557a57df44a46fb3ab72b6aafeaf945337/csrc/engine/input_metadata/prepare_tensor_input_base.cpp).

This is a released capability, not the stated production configuration: the deployment Q&A says CP and TP are not used there at present. DS4's prefill batching and bounded query work operate locally and do not constitute cross-device CP. Splitting one Mac's context into communicating processes would not supply another GPU's compute or bandwidth, so the mirror arrangement is not an automatic porting target.

## Replicating hot experts across ranks (P3)

A router can send disproportionate work to a few experts, making the slowest rank determine the step time. Expert-parallel load balancing (EPLB) gives a hot *logical* expert multiple identical physical copies on different ranks, routes work among them, and periodically changes placement based on recent token counts. The released [policy](https://github.com/deepseek-ai/DeepInfer/blob/553741557a57df44a46fb3ab72b6aafeaf945337/deepinfer/model_executor/eplb/policy.py) allocates replicas and places them to reduce historical rank peaks; [shuffle code](https://github.com/deepseek-ai/DeepInfer/blob/553741557a57df44a46fb3ab72b6aafeaf945337/deepinfer/model_executor/eplb/shuffle.py) moves weights. The production description gives prefill a 100-step statistics window and reassignment every 1,000 steps, versus 1,000 and 10,000 in decode. Its reported slowest-rank/mean loads are 1.074 for EP4 prefill, 1.045 for EP2 prefill and 1.090 for decode under its stated configurations. [Deployment EPLB](https://github.com/deepseek-ai/DeepInfer/blob/553741557a57df44a46fb3ab72b6aafeaf945337/doc/inference_deployment_qa.md#expert-parallel-load-balancing).

A second copy on the same GPU does not provide a second GPU's capacity. DS4's local expert residency and admission respond to storage and memory pressure instead. Its optional resident-expert substitution is a different approximation: it can use a *different* resident expert in place of a selected one, changing model computation, whereas upstream replicas retain the selected logical expert's weights. See [local routing](../apple-silicon.md#routing-and-metal-execution) for that distinction and its limits.

## Communication buffers and overlapping dispatch (P4)

EP must send token activations to their selected expert owners and return the weighted results. DeepInfer preallocates process-owned communication buffers, including symmetric buffers whose corresponding peer-visible storage supports direct exchange, and uses DeepEP operations to dispatch and combine across ranks. Asynchronous communication can overlap useful computation, but the transport and synchronization still cost time and memory. On NVIDIA, the buffer manager reserves streaming multiprocessors (SMs) for communication and reduces the SMs available to compute during that overlap; the tradeoff is not free parallelism. The public [buffer manager](https://github.com/deepseek-ai/DeepInfer/blob/553741557a57df44a46fb3ab72b6aafeaf945337/deepinfer/model_executor/parallel_utils/userbuffer.py) and [communication operations](https://github.com/deepseek-ai/DeepInfer/blob/553741557a57df44a46fb3ab72b6aafeaf945337/deepinfer/model_executor/parallel_utils/communication_op.py) expose these contracts.

NVLink, RDMA and DeepEP's communication SM allocation address cross-device transport and do not translate directly to Metal on a single Mac. The useful local principle is narrower: reuse suitably sized buffers, avoid unnecessary intermediate copies and produce data in a consumer-friendly layout. That principle alone does not establish an equivalent asynchronous distributed transport or a measured speedup.

## Separate vision, prefill and decode pools (P5)

Vision encoding, prompt prefill and token-by-token decode have different resource needs. The documented service separates their pools so it can provision them independently and balance time to first token against time between tokens. Its prefill machine chooses a vision-hosting machine at random, then selects the GPU on that machine processing the fewest image patches; prefill uses context-length tiers and compute-quota budgets, including a fitted cost for prompt chunk size and cached prefix length. Decode considers both request count and total KV length when assigning work. The production account recommends a roughly 1:1 prefill/decode GPU ratio and calls 64 prefill plus 64 decode GPUs the smallest efficient unit under its recommended EP64 decode, although 1P + 1D is deployable. [Deployment and load balancing](https://github.com/deepseek-ai/DeepInfer/blob/553741557a57df44a46fb3ab72b6aafeaf945337/doc/inference_deployment_qa.md#data-parallel-load-balancing).

The release contains much of the engine functionality needed for disaggregation, but its README explicitly excludes the complete integration and cross-replica prefill/decode balancing. DS4 has a native vision path and a local server; stage budgets and latency objectives are useful ways to reason about it, but separate cluster pools and their routing infrastructure are not required for a single computer. Local admitted model turns are synchronous, so the upstream service description should not be read as a claim of equivalent local scheduling. See [vision input](../implementation.md) and [server coordination](../../../../src/server/coordinator.zig).

## Network KV storage and recovery (P6)

A long-lived prefix cache lets a new request avoid recomputing a prior prompt. In the documented production service, main and indexer block KV live on a dedicated 3FS storage cluster, available to any prefill machine; this avoids forcing request routing to follow the machine holding a prefix. Storage nodes can scale independently of compute nodes, and a compute-node failure does not destroy the stored block KV. Sliding-window attention (SWA) state instead remains in the producing machine's host memory, with a directory for cross-machine retrieval. The account describes about 100 PiB of SSD, 3-day agent and 7-day chatbot TTLs, and 3FS garbage collection rather than expiry inside the cache store. Its 10+2 erasure coding tolerates two failures per group. These are production-scale descriptions, not guarantees of the public DeepInfer release. [Storage design](https://github.com/deepseek-ai/DeepInfer/blob/553741557a57df44a46fb3ab72b6aafeaf945337/doc/inference_deployment_qa.md#kv-cache-storage), [cluster storage](https://github.com/deepseek-ai/DeepInfer/blob/553741557a57df44a46fb3ab72b6aafeaf945337/doc/inference_deployment_qa.md#cluster--storage).

Prefix matching checks metadata before routing; a separate process asynchronously fetches matching blocks into host shared memory, and an engine thread swaps them into GPU memory while trying to overlap transfers with forward computation. A missing SWA state can be approximated by replaying the last 128 tokens while retaining the compressed blocks; the complete production cache-hit controller is not public. USRBIO (User Space Ring Based IO) lets an application enqueue I/O requests in shared-memory rings consumed by the 3FS FUSE process, with a shared data buffer for zero-copy transfers. It is an interprocess data path that bypasses ordinary FUSE data transfer; prefix matching and KV policy remain separate. DS4 does not need that interprocess path merely to access its local cache in one process. The separate [3FS USRBIO API reference](https://github.com/deepseek-ai/3FS/blob/main/src/lib/api/UsrbIo.md) describes these rings and buffers; it is outside the pinned DeepInfer checkout. DeepInfer's [release boundary](https://github.com/deepseek-ai/DeepInfer/blob/553741557a57df44a46fb3ab72b6aafeaf945337/README.md#L38-L42) excludes a complete cache system.

DS4 retains disposable inference checkpoints in local RAM and SSD, distinct from durable conversation ancestry. Its ordinary loader expects complete checkpoint state; the 128-row SWB1 recovery is a diagnostic prototype, not the production block-only admission policy. Local checkpoint materialization is synchronous. The [cache recipe](../../../../recipes/03-state-cache.md) and [comparison's recovery account](cache-and-attention.md#rebuilding-a-missing-recent-window-c1) explain the local behavior and limits.

## Scaling, request migration and failure recovery (P7)

Production autoscaling estimates prefill demand from uncached tokens and decode demand from running requests, with bounded, rate-limited adjustments and spare quota. During decode scale-in, an engine gets a one-minute grace period, then forwards unfinished requests and their KV state to other decode engines. This request migration prevents a long tail from keeping a large EP group alive. A failed GPU otherwise makes its whole communication group unusable; on restart the platform regroups the healthy GPUs and chooses the EP size from those available. The account says restarts take minutes and uses redundancy and rerouting to absorb them. [Autoscaling and fault tolerance](https://github.com/deepseek-ai/DeepInfer/blob/553741557a57df44a46fb3ab72b6aafeaf945337/doc/inference_deployment_qa.md#autoscaling--fault-tolerance).

DS4's local concern is reliable cancellation, session shutdown and restart from available durable history or checkpoints, not live migration of an in-flight request to another decode group. Restart and cache recovery have their own supported combinations and do not establish distributed failover, rank regrouping or uninterrupted generation. See the [Responses contract](../../../../src/serving/RESPONSES.md) and [local continuation](../implementation.md#continuation-and-storage).
