# Cache and attention in DeepInfer and DS4

A long-context continuation depends on more than its text. Attention reads recent raw keys and values, compressed history and index keys; compression can also have an unfinished pair, and selection has its own candidate state. DeepInfer and DS4 preserve the same trained model structure while making different storage and execution choices. The comparison below uses DeepInfer revision [`553741557a57df44a46fb3ab72b6aafeaf945337`](https://github.com/deepseek-ai/DeepInfer/tree/553741557a57df44a46fb3ab72b6aafeaf945337); the original DS4 audit used `e2a38f6e2bf4`, and later measurements identify their own revisions. Results on the local 1017 IQ2_XXS/Q2_K quant do not measure upstream's native MXFP4 checkpoint.

Three windows have different jobs. Encoder recovery can replay 128 positions to reconstruct missing sliding-window attention (SWA) after retaining compressed blocks. Decoder query compaction reduces work during prefill while preserving publication of the global history. A drafter needs a window of target features to propose future tokens after a restore. They are not interchangeable: each reconstructs or avoids a different part of the state. [Model state and continuation](../implementation.md#continuation-and-storage) describes the native ownership boundary; [sampling and scheduling](scheduling-and-speculation.md) covers verification itself.

## Rebuilding a missing recent window (C1)

DeepInfer's production description retains compressed and index blocks and replays the preceding 128 tokens when recent SWA state is missing. For a retained prefix ending at 32,768, replay starts at 32,640. At position `i`, raw visibility begins at `max(replay_start, i - 127)`; the missing older raw keys make this a bounded approximation, not a relocation of the suffix or an edit to the logical context. The public [input metadata](https://github.com/deepseek-ai/DeepInfer/blob/553741557a57df44a46fb3ab72b6aafeaf945337/csrc/engine/input_metadata/prepare_tensor_input_v4.cpp) includes the `swa_valid_start` floor, but the complete cache-hit controller described in the [deployment account](https://github.com/deepseek-ai/DeepInfer/blob/553741557a57df44a46fb3ab72b6aafeaf945337/doc/inference_deployment_qa.md) is not in the release.

DS4 calls its block-only diagnostic snapshot and importer SWB1. It preserves immutable blocks and reconstructs all 40 raw rings using 128 scalar replay rows. It demonstrates bounded recovery, not an isolated encoder-only recovery policy: ordinary SSD admission still requires complete checkpoint state, including raw rings, cumulative arrays, pending compressor state and logits. Full-state hits remain the established faster path. [The experiment](../../../../speed-bench/experiments/deepinfer-alignment-20261027/README.md) records its size, latency and baseline-trace changes. Batching those 128 rows and testing whether a block-only fallback improves retention under a bounded SSD working set are proposals; they do not justify replacing faster exact hits without measurement.

At the restored frontier, the runtime must know how many rows each shared owner has already published, so replay does not duplicate or overwrite compressed history. Candidate epochs determine whether a selection still belongs to the current published history. Compression in pairs can leave the first member pending at an odd anchor, and Engram needs the preceding three token IDs to reconstruct its input at replay start. Original positions, owner publication counts, candidate epochs, pending C2 pairs and that three-token history must therefore move together; rewinding only the graph position cannot reconstruct them.

[Existing DRP1 persistence](#persisting-approximate-replay-without-mistaking-it-for-exact-state-c13) applies to approximate decoder replay and excludes DSpark and suffix capture. A future persistent encoder-recovery approximation would need its own explicit decisions about these combinations; SWB1 does not establish them.

## Compacting decoder queries during prefill (C2)

In this model, encoder layers publish the shared compressed history that later decoder layers consume; decoder layers continue the computation using that history and their local window. See the [model reference](../reference-manual.md#sharing-and-candidate-selection). A query is a row of work for a token at a particular layer. During a long prefill, earlier decoder query rows can become unnecessary once they cannot affect the retained tail, even though the encoder must publish the complete compressed history. DeepInfer's [YOCO boundary](https://github.com/deepseek-ai/DeepInfer/blob/553741557a57df44a46fb3ab72b6aafeaf945337/deepinfer/model_executor/models/v4_modules/yoco.py) can physically compact those queries and their associated metadata; its [example](https://github.com/deepseek-ai/DeepInfer/blob/553741557a57df44a46fb3ab72b6aafeaf945337/examples/yoco/README.md) supports DFlash. Truncation defaults off with keep 0. The retained tail must cover the 128-token window plus speculative backtracking: default five drafts require at least 134 positions; 140 is an example, not a universal constant.

For eligible sweeps of at least 8,192 new rows, DS4 uses an exact shrinking dependency suffix: each decoder layer keeps the rows still needed to compute the retained result and its causal dependencies. Its optional fixed 128- or 256-row replay skips more work approximately. It requires replay and decoder suffix optimization to be enabled, at least 8,192 rows and more rows than the encoder chunk. It applies only to ordinary single-GPU text prefill without DSpark, images, importance-matrix collection or quality mode. Ordinary 512- and 2,048-row chunks do not activate this path. Both variants preserve full encoder/global-cache publication. See [prefill policy](../../../../src/engine/deepseek41/prefill_policy.zig), [prefill](../../../../src/engine/deepseek41/prefill.zig), and the [replay recipe](../../../../recipes/12-bounded-decoder-replay.md).

Extending compaction to ordinary chunks is a proposal, initially without DSpark; speculative MTP and rollback need a separate slice. A decision would require five alternating whole-request pairs, practical task quality, ΔNLL and KL. This proposal is not an instruction to start that experiment.

## Sharing physical compressed and index owners (C3)

Compressed history and index keys need not be separately stored by every layer that uses them. A publishing layer writes an owner's rows; later layers can read those same rows while computing their own queries and selection. DeepInfer derives these relationships from checkpoint metadata and validates the resulting topology in its [cross-layer implementation](https://github.com/deepseek-ai/DeepInfer/blob/553741557a57df44a46fb3ab72b6aafeaf945337/deepinfer/model_executor/models/v4_modules/cross_layer.py). DS4 implements the validated Flash topology rather than a general metadata-driven topology: layers 2, 8, 14 and 20 publish the physical compressed KV and index-key owners, while selection refreshes at 2, 8, 14, 20, 24, 28, 32 and 36. A later selection refresh is not necessarily a new physical owner. See [native configuration](../../../../src/engine/config.zig) and [sharing and selection](../reference-manual.md#sharing-and-candidate-selection).

## Reusing candidate blocks across layers (C4)

The indexer narrows which compressed rows final attention examines. For a long-context query at layer 20, it selects up to 2,048 candidate blocks of eight compressed rows; later refreshes score and select individual rows within the inherited blocks. The resulting IDs identify eligible history, not attention weights or the KV data itself. Final attention combines the selected main KV with the recent raw window. Candidate selection is part of the model's trained structure, not the separate optional resident-expert approximation. [DeepInfer's topology](https://github.com/deepseek-ai/DeepInfer/blob/553741557a57df44a46fb3ab72b6aafeaf945337/deepinfer/model_executor/models/v4_modules/cross_layer.py) checks source relationships and capacity; [DS4 attention](../../../../src/engine/deepseek41/attention.zig) applies its validated mapping.

## Scoring only eligible index keys (C5)

After a candidate source has excluded blocks, scoring keys outside them cannot affect the next selection. DeepInfer's [index attention](https://github.com/deepseek-ai/DeepInfer/blob/553741557a57df44a46fb3ab72b6aafeaf945337/deepinfer/model_executor/models/v4_modules/index_attn.py) can score inherited candidates in its consumers. DS4's opt-in `DS4_V41_CANDIDATE_INDEX=1` scalar path skips excluded keys after layer 20 once more than 16K keys exist, for both F32 and packed index storage. Batch prefill still scores the dense prefix before filtering and top-k.

The [candidate experiment](../../../../speed-bench/experiments/deepinfer-alignment-20261027/README.md) preserved distributions, selected/global row IDs and output token IDs on 32K and 64K probes and found a small warm-decode benefit, but no reliable first-answer improvement. A five-pair end-to-end comparison would be needed for a long-context decode promotion claim; the opt-in path is not evidence that batch prefill already avoids dense scores.

## Bounding score and top-k scratch (C6)

A long prefix can otherwise require a large temporary score matrix for every query. DeepInfer chunks index scoring according to row bytes and a memory budget, capped at 8,192 queries, before selection. DS4 keeps at most 32 live score rows and uses Metal partition/merge top-k, causal bounds and prefix optimizations. These are different implementations of bounded temporary work; replacing a presumed naive sort is not the first missing feature. See [upstream index attention](https://github.com/deepseek-ai/DeepInfer/blob/553741557a57df44a46fb3ab72b6aafeaf945337/deepinfer/model_executor/models/v4_modules/index_attn.py) and [local batch attention](../../../../src/engine/deepseek41/batch_attention.zig).

## Keeping history physically packed (C7)

Quantizing a value and expanding it back into F32 does not reduce the allocation. DeepInfer stores a raw SWA row of 512 values in 528 bytes as E4M3 FP8 plus per-32 UE8M0 scales; a 512-value compressed main row takes 288 bytes as E2M1 FP4 plus per-16 E4M3 scales; a 128-value index row takes 68 bytes as E2M1 FP4 plus per-32 UE8M0 scales. Its [cache format](https://github.com/deepseek-ai/DeepInfer/blob/553741557a57df44a46fb3ab72b6aafeaf945337/deepinfer/model_executor/layers/tilelang_ops/cache_common.py) makes the layout explicit. DS4 raw rows remain 2,048-byte F32; main rows can be F32, 1,024-byte F16 or physically packed 288-byte FP4, and index rows can be 512-byte F32 or physically packed 68-byte FP4.

The native producers retain original group-16 main and group-32 index scales; index publication uses mode-2 rounding. The 68-byte index producer/reader pilot checked its decoded values, scores and IDs against the old GPU quantizer and scorer. Scalar and batch consumers read the packed rows directly. On restore, the receiver may use a different layout from the saved state, so the importer converts older formats in bounded tiles without allocating a full expanded shadow. Legacy F32/F16 main values have already been decoded: repacking must preserve those values, including coarse subnormal scales where inferring the scale from `amax/6` alone fails.

Several checkpoint families carry these rows, with different compatibility rules:

- Complete canonical checkpoints use version 2 for expanded rows, version 3 for packed index, version 4 for packed main, and version 5 for both. Packed spans preserve their native bytes. F16 main owners still serialize main rows as F32, and raw disk rows remain F32.
- Diagnostic decoder-replay payloads (`DRP1`) and block-only restore payloads (`SWB1`) retain version-2 values through bounded conversion. Packing their live graph does not silently change that wire contract.
- Private approximate-suffix captures (`DSSD`) may use versions 2–5 while retaining approximate provenance. [C13](#persisting-approximate-replay-without-mistaking-it-for-exact-state-c13) explains why their identity must remain distinct from an exact prefix.

The [main recipe](../../../../recipes/15-packed-main-kv.md) provides the full compatibility matrix and qualification: SSD restore, drafter-history and verification state (DSP1/MTP), repeated suffix grafts, and a Responses restart into a graph with F32 main storage.

At context 262,144, four cumulative owners contain about 655,360 rows. Main plus index arrays calculate to approximately 1.5625 GiB for F32/F32, 1.2915 GiB for F32/packed, 0.6665 GiB for F16/packed and 0.2173 GiB for packed/packed, before alignment and scratch. These are capacity calculations, not whole-process memory or latency measurements. Smaller KV allocations do not automatically admit more experts: the freed headroom must reach the separately configured expert-cache admission budget. Physical index owners are 86.72% smaller than their F32 counterparts. The [SIMD follow-up](../../../../speed-bench/experiments/index-simd-20261027/README.md) retained exact fixture scores and IDs and reduced dense 256K FP4 scoring GPU time 39.2%, leaving roughly a 2% packed/F32 gap; that isolated kernel result is not a whole-task speedup.

In five alternating 32K pairs at revision `da31a6be3913`, fresh answer prefill plus 42 generated tokens averaged 48.730 s F32 and 48.362 s packed; order effects make this roughly speed-neutral, with matching teacher distributions and outputs. The [full samples and settings](../../../../speed-bench/experiments/index-five-samples-20261027/README.md) define the warm-model, fresh-KV scope. In a separate main-FP4 campaign using frozen revision `aee06ac9`, five 8K pairs were neutral and [five 32K pairs](../../../../speed-bench/experiments/main-fp4-20261028/RESULTS_32K.md) cost 1.98% more task time, slower in every pair, with identical observed teacher logits and output IDs; profiling located extra cost in indexed batch-attention consumption. These cohorts cannot be added together.

Main packing is selected with `DS4_V41_COMPRESSED_KV_FP4=1` before graph allocation. The opt-in DeepSeek fast presets enable physical main and index packing; ordinary engine defaults remain unchanged. Explicit main `=0` disables main packing, and an F16-only request chooses F16 unless main FP4 is explicitly `=1`. `persistent-packed` aliases `persistent-fast`. Physical raw FP8 remains a proposal: current mode 1 quantizes then expands to BF16-rounded F32, while retaining the original per-32 scales could reduce a raw row from 2,048 to 528 bytes. A useful experiment must show smaller allocation, decoded-value differences and consumer and whole-inference cost; packing alone does not imply a speedup.

## Rolling back speculative writes (C8)

A verifier can tentatively process several proposed tokens, then reject a suffix. Its cache must look as if those rejected positions had never been committed. DeepInfer allocates extra SWA and compressor ring space for backtracking in its [cache engine](https://github.com/deepseek-ai/DeepInfer/blob/553741557a57df44a46fb3ab72b6aafeaf945337/deepinfer/worker/cache_engine.py). DS4 instead saves overwritten raw and feature slots and compressor carries, restores them for rejected positions, and uses committed lengths to hide unaccepted compressed rows. Larger rings are therefore not inherently required for the local design. See [DSpark state handling](../../../../src/engine/deepseek41/dspark.zig) and the separate [verification account](scheduling-and-speculation.md).

## Separating durable blocks from disposable state (C9)

A reusable compressed prefix may outlive the recent per-request window. DeepInfer separates paged block KV from fixed state slots, allowing consumer state to be discarded and reconstructed; its deployment describes storing durable block KV on 3FS separately from SWA, with asynchronous fetch and garbage collection. The full production storage integration is omitted from the public release. See the [cache engine](https://github.com/deepseek-ai/DeepInfer/blob/553741557a57df44a46fb3ab72b6aafeaf945337/deepinfer/worker/cache_engine.py) and [deployment description](https://github.com/deepseek-ai/DeepInfer/blob/553741557a57df44a46fb3ab72b6aafeaf945337/doc/inference_deployment_qa.md).

DS4 identifies SSD chunks by their content and can deduplicate their arrays, but endpoint payloads still retain all 40 raw rings and compression carries at odd boundaries. SWB1 demonstrates reconstruction without establishing an ordinary eviction or admission policy. Durable conversation ancestry is also separate from disposable inference checkpoints: a cache hit accelerates continuation, but is not the source of truth. See [state payload](../../../../src/engine/deepseek41/state_payload.zig) and the [state-cache recipe](../../../../recipes/03-state-cache.md).

## Sharing GPU pages and forking state (C10)

DeepInfer's [block manager](https://github.com/deepseek-ai/DeepInfer/blob/553741557a57df44a46fb3ab72b6aafeaf945337/csrc/engine/block_manager.hpp) reference-counts GPU blocks and clones state slots so sequences can share a prefix while managing later writes. DS4 shares immutable stored SSD chunks and uses pins, but each restored graph owns private context-sized mutable GPU arrays. Stored-byte deduplication therefore does not supply GPU paging or copy-on-write forks. See [native resources](../../../../src/engine/deepseek41/resources.zig). GPU paging and copy-on-write remain larger projects, not prerequisites for the existing SSD cache.

## Fetching and importing cached state (C11)

Fetching a stored prefix involves choosing its blocks, reading and assembling bytes, and importing them into the live graph. DeepInfer's production description overlaps asynchronous fetch with inference, but its public release does not contain the whole storage service. 3FS USRBIO is the storage transport, not prefix matching, cache policy or a required single-process component; network transport is not a direct one-Mac optimization.

DS4 can overlap some expert and Engram I/O with computation, but checkpoint materialization currently assembles the entire endpoint synchronously before import. Unified memory removes a separate host-to-device tier, not SSD reads or assembly. See [state cache](../../../../src/state_cache.zig), [persistence import](../../../../src/server/persist.zig) and the [state-cache recipe](../../../../recipes/03-state-cache.md). Asynchronous checkpoint import remains a candidate, not an implemented capability.

## Restoring useful drafter history (C12)

A restored target cache alone may leave a drafter without the recent target features needed to propose tokens, forcing a warmup before verified drafting. DeepInfer's [DFlash](https://github.com/deepseek-ai/DeepInfer/blob/553741557a57df44a46fb3ab72b6aafeaf945337/deepinfer/model_executor/models/mtp/dflash.py) projects target features into drafter attention KV during forward. DS4 instead persists the exact raw BF16 target-feature window as a DSP1 trailer in canonical v2–v5; on first use it rebuilds projected drafter KV. Both approaches avoid waiting for 128 future scalar target tokens, but their stored representations differ. See [local speculative code](../../../../src/engine/deepseek41/speculative.zig).

At ordinary Metal confidence 0.6, using frozen revision `9446a21d`, [five alternating pairs](../../../../speed-bench/experiments/draft-features-five-20261028/README.md) reduced total HTTP time for the three restored tasks by 18.12%, with matching outputs and 10.343 MiB additional stored files across three endpoints. This is a restore-workload result, not a general decoding speedup; the earlier eager-policy measurements are separate. Fast presets enable `DS4_V41_CHECKPOINT_DRAFT_FEATURES=1` and honor an explicit 0; ordinary engine defaults are unchanged, and the trailer only helps when DSpark is used.

## Persisting approximate replay without mistaking it for exact state (C13)

A checkpoint created after approximate decoder replay carries different history from an exact full-state checkpoint, even if its visible text is the same. DS4 records a typed DRP1 replay policy and approximate identity, permits matching-prefix resume, and excludes canonical export and suffix grafts for this replay kind. This is deliberate approximation-aware persistence, not an accidental leak into an exact cache. The [checkpoint policy](../../../../src/engine/deepseek41/checkpoint.zig) and [replay recipe](../../../../recipes/12-bounded-decoder-replay.md) define the boundary; upstream's documented decoder replay cache policy is stricter.

DSpark and suffix capture are excluded from this replay path, while exact packed-main checkpoints have separately qualified SSD, DSP1/MTP and repeated suffix-graft combinations. Do not infer that qualification transfers to approximate replay. Semantic endpoint reuse for edited contexts also has its own exclusions; see [edited contexts](../implementation.md#edited-contexts-and-semantic-endpoints).
